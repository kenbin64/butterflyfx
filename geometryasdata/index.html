Geometry as Data

Data stores every value. Geometry stores the rule that makes the values, plus the small residual it can't predict, and rebuilds the rest on demand.

Below it runs on a real sound file and a real image - every number computed live in your browser. Three parts each: the pattern that repeats, the rule that generates it, the residual left over. What you save is exactly the structure that was there. No structure, no win, no magic.

example one · sound is geometry

A wave, seen as itself

A WAV stores the speaker's position tens of thousands of times a second. But sound is waves - and a wave is the shadow of a spinning helix. Look down that helix and a pure tone is a single point on a circle. That is where the sound's real shape lives.

Pattern

The same few tones and harmonics repeat through the entire track.

Rule

Each wave is sin/cos at one frequency - one small equation per tone.

Residual

The little the pure waves miss; the quiet detail you shed when you go small.

example two · a picture is mostly its edges

Rule paints, residual is the lines

Store every pixel and you're hoarding data. But most of a picture is smooth - so a rule guesses each pixel from its neighbors, and only the edges it can't guess are kept.

Pattern

The smooth fill - skies, walls, gradients - areas that follow their neighbors.

Rule

A predictor: each pixel ≈ a blend of the pixels already known around it.

Residual

Only the edges the rule missed. That grey emptiness is geometry, kept free.

the honest part

The savings are exactly the geometry

Read a recording as geometry and it beats a normal compressor many times over, because a normal compressor sees bytes, not waves. Read a structured image and the residual is just the edges. Read pure noise and the residual is the whole thing - nothing saved. That boundary is the proof it isn't a trick.

Send-to-a-friend version: data stores every value; geometry stores the rule plus a small leftover and rebuilds the rest. Sound is waves - most of a whole track lives in a tiny slice of its frequencies. An image is mostly smooth - keep a rule that predicts each pixel and only the edges it missed. Random noise saves nothing. You save exactly as much as there was structure.
