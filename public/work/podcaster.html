A podcast studio where the episode is finished when you stop talking.

ButterflyFx Podcast Creator lets you write the visuals into the script. Every graphic, caption, transition and effect appears on the exact word you say, while a teleprompter keeps that word right under the camera so you are always looking at the lens. No post-production.

The problem

Most video podcasts are recorded first and dressed afterwards. Someone scrubs through the footage, finds the moment the host mentions the chart, drops the chart in, and does that again for every caption and transition. It takes hours, and the pictures rarely land on the word that needs them. Meanwhile the host reads from notes off to the side, so they look like they are reading.

What it does

  • The script carries the show. A cue in curly braces is something the audience sees, and it fires on the word right after it. A note in square brackets is a direction for the host alone.
  • A teleprompter built for the lens. The current word sits right under the camera, with the next words beside it, so the host's eyes stay on the lens.
  • A director for takes. It splits the script into scenes, waits until the host's delivery is in range, slates and records each take, and assembles the keepers with transitions.
  • AI writing on your own machine. Describe the topic and a local model writes a script in the cue format, or rewrites a scene on request. Nothing leaves the computer.

Here is part of the script behind the demo above:

A cue names {text ll | Where it goes} where it goes, {cleartext} {text ll | Which asset} which asset to show,
{cleartext} {text ll | A short label} and a short label.
{clear} {news | placement.svg | Cue before the word} The one rule: put the cue right before its word,
and the picture lands the moment you say it.

How the demo was made

The narration is a Microsoft neural voice, which reports the exact moment each word begins. Those moments drove the studio the same way its voice-follow mode tracks a live speaker: each word advanced the teleprompter and fired that word's cues, and each narrated step clicked through the real interface, while the browser recorded the screen. The same steps can turn any script and a folder of pictures into a finished episode, which is how I plan to make demo videos of my other projects.

The proof

  • All 41 cues in the demo script fired on their words, across 134 spoken words, with no page errors.
  • The studio loads with no page errors in Chrome, and its launcher installs Electron and starts it, loading the demo episode from the repository's own folder.
  • The full user guide, the cue reference and the writing guide are in the repository, along with the demo episode and the pipeline that recorded it.

Technologies and skills

  • JavaScript, Canvas 2D and Web Audio: a 1920 by 1080 compositor, synthesized effects and an adaptive score.
  • Electron and Node.js: a local-only desktop shell and file server.
  • MediaPipe: on-device background removal.
  • MediaRecorder: takes, assembly and export to WebM.
  • Ollama: local AI for writing and rewriting scripts.
  • AI-assisted engineering with Claude Code, under my design and direction.

What it does not claim

  • Export is WebM only, and AI video generation is not built yet.
  • Following the host's voice depends on the browser's speech recognition, which may not be available in the desktop shell.
  • The demo's host and voice are synthetic; a live episode uses your camera and microphone.
