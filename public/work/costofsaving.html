A saving you can see, beside the cost you can't.

Cost of Saving is a set of free calculators for money decisions: whether a purchase is worth it, what a saving really costs, whether a business cut still saves once every cost is counted, what a job really pays, and what putting something off will cost. I designed it and made every decision; an AI wrote the code and tests to my standards.

The problem

A saving is usually recorded in one place and what it costs in another, so nobody adds them up. A cheaper supplier shows up as a saving this quarter; the returns, the rework and the lost customer show up later, somewhere else. The same happens at home: doing it yourself, waiting on a repair, taking the job with the longer commute.

Who did what

  • Me: the idea that every saving has a hidden cost; the questions the site asks (what it costs to spend, to cut, to earn and to wait, and the cost of adding steps); what each calculator asks; the examples; a rule of fairness, such as the on-site, hybrid and work-from-home comparison showing three examples with three different results; and every decision about what stays.
  • The AI (Claude, in VS Code): the calculators, the pages, the build that turns them into the site, the tests, and the small server pieces on Cloudflare, with a plain report of what it checked and what it did not.

How the work was kept honest

  • Nothing counts as working until it has been run.
  • Tests that try to break it: 13 test suites and 517 automated checks, with sums worked by hand, the edges, and tipping points fed back in to prove they flip the answer.
  • An independent second version. The main calculator is checked against a second version of itself, so one mistake cannot hide behind another.
  • Every formula published with a worked example you can follow on a pocket calculator.
  • 117 pages, every link checked on every build; a broken link stops the build.
  • Readable without scripts: the answers are also written into each page as plain text, from the same data, so search engines and AI assistants read what a person sees.

Why it matters to an employer

  • AI-assisted development done properly: fast output from the AI, with direction, standards and checks from a person.
  • Business judgment: real decisions turned into numbers a manager can act on.
  • Evidence over assertion: published formulas, automated checks and independent verification, the same habits regulated work depends on.
  • Privacy by design: no accounts and no stored data; every sum runs on the person's device.

Technologies and skills

  • JavaScript with no frameworks or dependencies; each calculator is a plain, testable module.
  • Node.js tests and a build that generates the whole site, its sitemap and structured data.
  • Cloudflare Workers and KV: the feedback form, which emails me, and a cookie-free visit count.
  • Installable web app: it installs from the site and works offline (service worker).
  • SEO and structured data, accessible and responsive design, and charts drawn from the data.
  • AI-assisted engineering with Claude Code in VS Code, under written standards.

What it does not claim

  • It is a personal portfolio piece and skills demonstration, not a commercial product: free, with no ads, no cookies and no sign-in.
  • It is arithmetic on your own estimates, not financial advice or a prediction.
