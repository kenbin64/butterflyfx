Fabric of Space

The Sun and eight planets on their real orbits at their real speeds, moving through the field their masses make. Height shows how deep the field is at each point. The whole system is also crossing the Milky Way at 230 km/s, so each planet leaves a coil behind it like a wake, rising out of the disc 30.4° from exactly perpendicular.

How this model is built, and what it is an estimate of
  • Orbits are real ellipses in three dimensions, tilted against each other and set against the galactic plane, from JPL's approximate orbital elements with their slow changes. Each planet speeds up near the Sun. These elements are meant for 1800 to 2050, and the positions were checked against JPL's own positions. They do not carry the planets' short-term tugs on each other.
  • The field is Newton's sum: each body contributes its mass divided by its distance. At these speeds and distances, general relativity changes this by only parts in 100 million, which the warp reading shows.
  • The funnel is a graph, not a shape in space. The planets lie in a flat disc, and the funnel's height shows field strength at each point of that disc. The depth axis is not a direction you could travel. Slide "Flat disc" to see this: the planets keep their places while the height goes away. Around the Sun, the field itself is close to round in all directions, like nested shells.
  • Each coil marks where a planet has been, trailing behind it like a wake. It is the path itself, and nothing physical is left along it in this model.
  • "Time, perpendicular" is a spacetime diagram: the disc is space, and the axis square to it is time, so each coil is a planet's worldline, the record of where it was at each moment. The past lies back along the axis. Its true scale is one light year of axis per year, 63,241 AU, which makes the coils almost straight. The time axis is a way of drawing time and is not a direction in space. "Sun's path in space" is the other view: a real direction, 30.4 degrees from perpendicular to the disc.
  • "Spiral drawn flat on it" is a depiction added to the sheet: a logarithmic spiral with a ratio of 6.5 per turn, chosen to show well. The sum over the masses does not produce it, and the planets do not fall in. The coils are the calculated spirals.
  • The coils are calculated: each planet's real orbit, tilted by the measured angle, carried along the Sun's path. Only "True ratio" keeps the true proportion between the Sun's travel and the orbits, where the coils are nearly straight lines. "Slowed to see coils" shortens the travel and squeezes distances so the shape can be seen.
  • The Sun's path is drawn straight, since it bends only about 1 degree per 600,000 years. Its speed and the distance to the center are standard round values. Its small drift relative to nearby stars and its slow bob through the galactic plane are left out.
  • The sheet is a slice through Earth's orbital plane, and the other planets rise slightly off it by their true tilts. The real field fills all of space around the Sun.
  • Height is drawn on a logarithmic scale, because the field is far too steep near the Sun to show otherwise. "Squeezed" distances are logarithmic too, so straight sides on the funnel are expected from that scale.
  • "Enlarged to see" makes each planet's dip deep enough to notice, ordered by mass but not to scale. The numbers in the table are always the true ones.
  • The Sun stays at the center, though it really shifts a little as the planets pull it. Bodies are drawn far larger than true scale. Earth's map is a hand-drawn simplified outline.
  • "Watch Earth turn" runs one day in 6 seconds, with Earth spinning at its true rate against its orbit: about 361 degrees a day while it moves about 1 degree along its path. At faster speeds a true spin would only flicker, so it is slowed to 24 turns per year.
