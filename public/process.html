From the first message to the live address

The work is on the other pages. This is how it gets made.

Every project in this portfolio was built the same way: I direct, an AI coding agent writes and tests, and nothing is called finished until it has been run and checked on the live address.

This page shows the whole process, end to end: the workbench, the instructions the AI works under, where the work is hosted and why it moved, how each change is verified, and the conversations that took a project further than the first answer would have.

Ordinary tools, used deliberately. Nothing here is exotic. The difference is in how they are set up and what the AI is required to do with them.

From a server to a platform

The projects began on a self-managed Linux server: nginx in front, services under systemd, one machine to patch and watch. Most of them now run on Cloudflare, and the move was done project by project with the AI doing the work and me making the calls.

ThenNowWhat changed
Static sites served by nginxCloudflare static assetsNo server to patch, served from the edge, deployed with one command.
Small back-end servicesCloudflare WorkersA contact form, a feedback form and a visit counter, each a few dozen lines, each with its own tests.
A database for small recordsWorkers key-value storageOnly what a feature needs, for example one daily count to stop a form being abused.
Email from a mail serverCloudflare Email Routing and outbound emailA visitor's message reaches me without my address appearing in any page.
Domains at another registrarDomains moved to CloudflareRegistration, DNS and hosting in one place, at cost.

Not everything has moved. The two Python services, citeline and mcp-gateway, need a host that runs Python and Postgres. Their case studies say plainly that the live demos are offline, and the source and tests are public in the meantime.

How a change is checked

The rule is the same for every project: the AI does not report a change as done until it has run the evidence and shown it to me.

Tests first

Every calculation has a case worked by hand, the edges are tried, and the test suite runs before anything is published. Cost of Saving runs 517 checks on every build.

Then the real page

A real browser opens the built site, clicks through it, and measures it: links, layout on a phone, whether a 3D tour ever walks through a wall.

Then the live address

After deploying, the published copy is checked again, file by file, against what was built.

Then my eyes

A test proves what it was written to prove. Whether the thing is right for the person using it is my judgment, and I make it by using it.

The conversations that went further

The first answer is rarely the best one. These are real exchanges, my words quoted with only the spelling tidied, where pushing back or asking one more question changed what got built.

A hypothesis tested, not trusted

I asked
We are forcing AI to be like humans that require high-level languages. AI could talk directly to the CPU, or at least a lower-level language.
What we found
Instead of agreeing or arguing, the AI measured it. The same small program cost 14 tokens in Python and 138 in assembly. Lower level was more expensive, not less.
What it gave
A better question: what is the least an AI needs to be told to still get it right? That became Least Tokens, a benchmark of 3,600 answers from three models.

The test that was looking at the wrong thing

I asked
So far no traction, so it could be the fault of the resume not being read.
What we found
The AI read the resume the way an applicant tracking system does, by extracting its text. Dates were merged, jobs were out of order and part of the history was missing, all of which a parser can stumble on.
What it gave
A rebuilt resume in plain, machine-readable form, checked by pulling the text back out of the PDF to confirm every date and keyword survived.

Working, tested, and still not right

I asked
Is there any way to make the 3D part look more like an art gallery and not just hallways with art?
What we found
The hallways passed every test. They were still the wrong thing: no test can tell you a space does not feel like a gallery.
What it gave
A rebuild into rooms joined by doorways, with benches, track lighting and room names. The gallery's own page records the 67 messages of direction behind its first three days.

Checking my own argument before I published it

I asked
Evaluate it, fact-check it, and make it conform with the facts.
What we found
On an article I had drafted about business cost-cutting, the AI checked each claim against the record. The headline claim did not hold. The point underneath it did.
What it gave
A rewritten article that keeps my argument and could survive a knowledgeable reader. An assistant that only agreed with me would have let me publish the first version.

Saying so when something is offline

I asked
See what is in mcp-gateway.
What we found
The project was strong, with 29 passing tests, lint and type checks, and barely visible. Its "live" link, and citeline's, now led nowhere after the server move.
What it gave
Both moved to the top of this portfolio, with notices saying the demos are offline and how to run them, in place of links that would have failed in front of a reader.

Who does what

Stated the same way on everything I publish.

I conceive, direct and decide. What gets built, who it is for, what must stay true, and when it is finished.

The AI writes and tests. It produces the code, runs it, and reports what was and was not checked. It does not originate the ideas, and I do not claim to have typed every line.

The record is open. The source is on GitHub, the tests can be run, and each site says how it was built.
